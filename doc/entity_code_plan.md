# Entity codes and seed manifests for Ascent

Ascent's seeds are moving onto the MICharisma Inc entity-code design shared with AthleteAce: every seeded record carries a structured, catalog-relative code, and reference data is replayed from `micharisma-seeds/v1` manifests. The design and its decision record live in AthleteAce: [entity-codes-and-seed-library-proposal.md](https://github.com/mikeymicrophone/AthleteAce/blob/codex/yaml-seasonal-seeding/docs/entity-codes-and-seed-library-proposal.md), [entity-code-decisions.md](https://github.com/mikeymicrophone/AthleteAce/blob/codex/yaml-seasonal-seeding/docs/entity-code-decisions.md), and the [Ascent adapter sketch](https://github.com/mikeymicrophone/AthleteAce/blob/codex/yaml-seasonal-seeding/docs/ascent-seed-adapter-sketch.md). This document records how those ideas map onto Ascent's schema and the decisions made for Ascent on October 20, 2026.

## Elections, races, and the design's "Election"

The design was written before Ascent separated races from elections. Its "Election" is one contest for one office, which is an Ascent **Race**. An Ascent **Election** is a dated voting event in one jurisdiction; Ascent keeps one Election per jurisdiction per contest, and each event has its own code.

| Ascent record | Code | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Election (event in one jurisdiction) | `ELECTION-{jurisdiction path}-{contest key}` | `ELECTION-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125-2026_GENERAL` |
| Race (contest for one office) | `RACE-{office path}-{contest key}` | `RACE-USA-NY-COUNTY_ERIE-SHERIFF-2026_GENERAL` |
| Candidacy | `CANDIDACY-{office path}-{contest key}-{username}` | `CANDIDACY-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125-2026_GENERAL-{username}` |
| JurisdictionBoundary (the design's district map edition) | `BOUNDARY-{jurisdiction path}-MAP_{year}` | `BOUNDARY-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125-MAP_2024` |

- **Jurisdiction path:** each place's code without its type marker. `STATE-USA-NY` has the path `USA-NY`; `DISTRICT-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125` has `USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125`. A county's path adds `COUNTY_` (`COUNTY-USA-NY-ERIE` has the path `USA-NY-COUNTY_ERIE`), so it never collides with a town of the same name, such as Hartford, Connecticut.
- **Office path:** the jurisdiction path, plus a position token when the jurisdiction holds more than one office (`USA-NY-GOVERNOR`, `USA-NY-COUNTY_ERIE-SHERIFF`). A district that elects one seat needs none. Local offices add the printed title and any seat (`USA-NY-AURORA-TOWN_SUPERVISOR-TOWN_OF_AURORA`), except in a District drawn for that one office, which already carries the title. Judicial seats add the position and the group or retention seat (`USA-FL-CIRCUIT-CIR_1-CIRCUIT_JUDGE-GROUP_10`, `USA-FL-APPEAL-DCA_1-APPELLATE_JUDGE-RETENTION_OF_JOSEPH_LEWIS_JR`).
- **Contest key:** year and Election kind, such as `2026_GENERAL` or `2026_SPECIAL`. A special election is its own Election even when it shares a date and jurisdiction with a general one, so the converter needs source-backed rules for marking specials (Florida's SD 21 special, Virginia's HD 20 special, and Virginia's "- Special" local offices).
- Certification status (`certified`, `tentative`, `unofficial`) stays out of every code, as the decision record requires.

Other codes follow the design: `COUNTRY-USA`, `STATE-USA-NY`, `COUNTY-USA-NY-ERIE`, `MUNICIPALITY-USA-CT-HARTFORD`, `DISTRICT-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125`, `POSITION-STATE_SENATOR`, `OFFICE-{office path}`, and `PERSON-{username}`. Local office path shapes should be settled on real rows during conversion.

**Nominations and organizations.** A primary is a race for a nomination, and a nomination is an Office: position Nominee, the nominated office as `nominee_for`, the party or placeholder Organization that nominates, and how many advance (`nominee_count`: 1, or 2 for a top-two primary). Its code adds `NOMINEE_{organization}` to the nominated office's path: `OFFICE-USA-GA-CONGRESS-CD_5-NOMINEE_DEMOCRATIC`, raced as `RACE-USA-GA-CONGRESS-CD_5-NOMINEE_DEMOCRATIC-2026_PRIMARY`. A race's office may sit anywhere within its election's jurisdiction, so one state primary holds every district's nominations. Parties are Organizations (`Party < Organization`) and keep `PARTY-USA-GA-DEMOCRATIC`; other organizations, from unions to nonpartisan-primary placeholders, take `ORGANIZATION-{jurisdiction path}-{NAME}`. After each import, `Race.gather_primary_candidates!` gives everyone still running in a general's primaries an uncoded `off_ballot` candidacy in that general, pointing back at their `primary_candidacy`, so voters can rate a candidate the primary eliminated. Manifests never set `primary_candidacy`. If a manifest later places that person in the general, the importer claims the off-ballot candidacy by its natural key and keeps its ratings.

Codes use uppercase letters, digits, dashes, and underscores, so a Person's code uppercases its lowercase username (`PERSON-JAMES_LT_6J`). Years are not coded: `YEAR-2024` names the year itself.

## Storage

- **`entity_code` columns** hold each record's preferred code, catalog-relative (the `MIC-ASC-` prefix comes from configuration), with a unique index on each coded table.
- **`entity_codes`** (ported from AthleteAce's `EntityCode`) registers every issued code, preferred and alias, against its record's current preferred code. Registering preferred codes as well as aliases gives one uniqueness check across all types in the catalog and one place to resolve any code.

## Person usernames

A person's username is a version of their name and a disambiguator issued to everyone in advance, as Signal does: Letitia James is `james_lt_6j`, so her code is `PERSON-JAMES_LT_6J`. A username names no state or office, because a person may later run somewhere else. The rules:

- **Name code:** the surname, then the given name's first letter and its next consonant (Letitia → `lt`, Clyde → `cl`, Ann → `an`). A person printed with only a surname has the surname alone (`healey`). Middle names, nicknames, and suffixes are omitted; accents become plain letters and punctuation is removed.
- **Disambiguator:** a digit and a letter drawn at random, skipping the look-alikes 0, 1, I, and O, so each name code has 192. It is checked against every username issued in any state and any person code in a contribution, committed in the state's `usernames.yml`, and reused on every replay.
- **Submissions** may use the name code alone: `PERSON-JAMES_LT` resolves to the one person whose code adds a disambiguator to it. When two people share the name code, the importer refuses it and lists both full codes.
- Social media handles belong to the person's Channel records, not their username.
- `username_status` stays `provisional` until an update request confirms the person's identity.

Open: how conflicting update requests are resolved.

## Ballot lines and endorsements

Decided October 21, 2026. A printed ballot line is an official fact; an endorsement is voluntary support. They are separate records, linked where they overlap.

- **BallotLine:** one per printed line on a Candidacy, recording the label exactly as the source prints it. Its kind is `party`, `designation` (Massachusetts labels such as "Gulf War Veteran"), `petition` (Connecticut's "Petitioning Candidate"), `write_in`, or `unclassified`; only a party line references a Party. `unclassified` marks a printed label whose kind has not been established from the state's list of parties, which is how the current row seeder records every label other than write-ins and petitions until conversion classifies them. A fusion candidate keeps one Candidacy, the single thing voters approve, with several BallotLines. BallotLines are refreshed from official sources and carry no codes of their own, consistent with the decision record's exclusion of coded `BallotLine` entities.
- **Endorsement:** an endorser (a Party or Person today; Organization once it is modeled) supporting a Candidacy, with the strength and dates planned in the approval-voting PRD's multi-endorsement party tools. A party can endorse several candidates in one race, and can endorse where it has no line, such as Florida's nonpartisan judicial races.
- **The link:** a party's BallotLine also counts as that party's endorsement. It appears as an Endorsement of kind `ballot_line`, created and refreshed with its BallotLine, so a party's endorsements include its fusion lines. Endorsements of other kinds are never changed by a seed refresh of the official lines.
- **Party:** a ballot-qualified party within a state, coded `PARTY-{state path}-{party}` (for example `PARTY-USA-NY-WORKING_FAMILIES`). The same name in two states is two Parties.

## Phases

1. **Foundation (done October 20, 2026).**
   - `lib/micharisma_seeders` is a submodule of [micharisma_seeders](https://github.com/mikeymicrophone/micharisma_seeders), pinned to `main`. It is excluded from `autoload_lib`, and `MICharisma` is an inflection acronym. AthleteAce pins a later commit on `codex/published-seed-schemas` that adds schema-export tooling.
   - `AddEntityCodes` adds `entity_code` to countries, states, counties, municipalities, districts, positions, offices, elections, races, people, and candidacies; creates `entity_codes`; and adds `people.username`, `people.username_status`, and `offices.seat_code`.
2. **Importer (done October 21, 2026).**
   - `Seeds::LibraryImporter` is ported from AthleteAce as Ascent's persistence adapter for the coded types above. It resolves references by code or alias, retries records until their dependencies exist, creates missing records, adopts uncoded records created by the reference seeders through natural keys (for example a Position's title), preserves differing existing values and reports them as conflicts, and registers preferred codes and aliases. `bin/rails seed_library:validate` and `seed_library:import` read `db/seeds/ascent_seed_data/manifests`, or the folder named by `SEED_LIBRARY_ROOT`.
   - A jurisdiction reference names the owning place's code (`jurisdiction: DISTRICT-USA-NY-ASSEMBLY-AD_125`); a place imported without a Jurisdiction gets one nested under its parent (a District's parent comes from its `state` scope).
   - BallotLines have no codes, so a manifest lists them on their Candidacy as `ballot_lines`, each with a `label` and optional `kind` and `party` code. Lines are added, never removed; a differing existing line is reported.
   - `AddBallotLinesAndEndorsements` creates `parties`, `ballot_lines`, and `endorsements`, adds `candidacies.participation` (`active`, `inactive`, `off_ballot`) and `races.certification_status` (`certified`, `tentative`, `unofficial`), and removes `candidacies.party_affiliation`, refusing to run while any candidacy still has a value there. Views show `Candidacy#ballot_labels`; the candidacy form edits participation instead of a party string.
   - The [Kelles manifest](../spec/fixtures/seed_library/kelles_2024.yml) imports as one Candidacy with two BallotLines and two `ballot_line` Endorsements, and re-importing it changes nothing.
   - Entity-sequence lookups are refused with a clear error until Phase 3 installs their recipes.
3. **Converter and New York pilot (done October 21, 2026).**
   - `Seeds::BallotManifestConverter` (`bin/rails seed_library:convert STATE=new_york SELECTION=pilot`) reads a state's federal and state rows, `conflicts.csv`, and `conversion/2026/<state>/rules.yml` (named `parties.yml` until Phase 4), and writes `manifests/foundation.yml` (the country and positions, shared by every state), `manifests/2026/<state>/*.yml`, and three files in `conversion/2026/<state>/`: `usernames.yml` (issued usernames, reused on every run), `name_spellings.yml`, and `judgments.md`.
   - One Candidacy per person per contest, with a BallotLine for each printed line. A label listed among the state's parties becomes a `party` line linked to a Party; write-ins and petitions keep those kinds; any other label takes the state's `other_labels` kind. New York's qualified parties are Democratic, Republican, Conservative, and Working Families; other New York labels belong to independent bodies nominated by petition and convert as `designation`.
   - People whom a cross-check source lists but the official source does not are included with participation `off_ballot` and no ballot line. Name and party conflicts and incumbency disagreements are reported, not applied.
   - Certification status maps `certified`, `tentative`/`subject to change`, and `unofficial`; any other source wording is left blank and reported. A contest is a special election when its office or notes say so.
   - The New York pilot converts 28 contests: the four statewide contests; the four most contested districts for U.S. House, State Senate, and Assembly; and every contest in those offices with a logged conflict. It imports as 264 coded records with 73 candidacies (5 off the ballot), 116 ballot lines, and 101 party endorsements, and re-imports without change. On top of the reference seeders it adopts their country, states, and positions. Its judgments report is `conversion/2026/new_york/judgments.md` in the seed-data repository.
   - The `csv` gem is now a direct dependency; Ruby 4 no longer loads it by default.
4. **Full conversion (done October 21, 2026).**
   - The converter handles every seat in the five states' rows: Massachusetts's named legislative districts, Governor's Council and judicial districts, Florida's judicial circuits with group seats, and judges standing for merit retention, who each get their own race. Local offices land in their town (inside its county when there is exactly one), their county, or a District named for the office and the places it covers. Source contests that resolve to the same race are merged, and a candidate listed in both keeps one username.
   - Each state's `conversion/2026/<state>/rules.yml` sets its parties, label kinds, labels that are never printed, and the patterns that mark special elections:
     - New York: Democratic, Republican, Conservative, and Working Families are qualified parties; other labels are independent bodies, converted as designations. Source: [Qualified New York political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualified_New_York_political_parties) (secondary).
     - Connecticut: the CTEMS major and minor parties; "Petitioning Candidate" is a petition line and Berlin's "Non Partisan" a designation.
     - Massachusetts: Democratic and Republican (with the list's abbreviation "Demo") are the recognized parties; every other label is a political designation. Source: the Secretary of the Commonwealth's [Candidates Guide 2026](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/download/getting-on-the-ballot/Candidates-Guide-2026.pdf).
     - Virginia: Democratic, Republican, Libertarian, and Green lines, from the Department of Elections' ELECT-613 ballot-label sign; whether the Libertarian and Green parties meet Virginia's recognized-party test for 2026 is not confirmed. Local rows get no line because Virginia prints party only for federal, statewide, and General Assembly offices.
     - Florida: all eight parties in the data are registered with the [Division of Elections](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/candidates-committees/political-parties/); "No Party Affiliation (Partisan)" is a designation, and the nonpartisan-office label is never printed. Unopposed candidates, who do not appear on Florida's ballot, are still converted as active candidacies; Ascent's slates need not follow ballot practice that precisely.
     - Special elections are marked only where a source marks them: Virginia's "- Special" titles and unexpired-term notes, and Florida's SD 21 special (DOS election `20261103-S01`).
   - Converted with `SELECTION=all`, the five states produce 4,161 races in 1,493 elections, 6,822 candidacies (12 off the ballot), 3,732 ballot lines (2,861 party lines, each also a party endorsement), 23 parties, 1,460 counties, municipalities, and districts, and 1,283 positions: 26,231 coded records. The judgments report for each state lists its remaining open decisions.
   - `db:seed` now imports the manifests through `SeedManifests`, foundation files first. The governor seeder creates only the races the manifests lack, so New York's, Connecticut's, Massachusetts's, and Florida's governor races keep their official-source descriptions. Importing all five states into an empty database takes about three minutes (184 seconds, about 200,000 queries), and re-importing them takes about 30 seconds with no changes. During an import the importer holds the catalog's code bindings and the records it has resolved in memory instead of querying, and row-locking, each one; that cut the first import from about 15.5 minutes and the re-import from about 13. Because the per-binding row locks are gone, two imports should not run against one database at the same time. A full `seed_reference!` (countries, states, `cities.yml`, positions, years, manifests, then the governor seeder) takes about three minutes and yields 4,193 races, including 36 governor races with no duplicate offices; the manifests adopt the reference seeders' country, states, positions, and municipalities. Its one reported conflict is St. Petersburg, Florida, which `cities.yml` creates without a county; the existing value is kept. Positions already defined in `positions.yml` are referenced by title only, so their curated descriptions are never contested.
   - `BallotLineSeeder` is retired. Its row reading, name splitting, and office mapping live in `Seeds::BallotRows`, which only the converter uses.
5. **Position catalog (done October 28, 2026).**
   - `manifests/foundation.yml` is now hand-curated: the country and every coded Position, loaded before any other manifest. It replaces `positions.yml` and `PositionSeeder`. A Position is a role, such as Mayor or County Executive; its Offices supply the place. Other data files name Positions by code, and the converter no longer creates any.
   - Each state's `conversion/2026/<state>/offices.yml` maps every printed local title to a Position, plus any seat, special district (`body`), town, or partial `term` the title prints. A title missing from it stops the conversion. Local titles had become 1,283 one-off Positions, such as "Opa-locka - Mayor" and "SUPERVISOR-BEDFORD". The 1,269 printed titles now share 59 local Positions, and the catalog has 76 in all.
   - A district column that prints the office's own town or county names its place, not a seat. When a row's jurisdiction is only a county, a district printed as "Town of X" makes the town the place. This moves Virginia's town mayors and councils out of their counties. A bare "County" jurisdiction defers to the row's county.
   - Special districts, such as Florida's community development, fire, and soil and water districts, share a Position for their kind. The district's name starts the office's seat, and the office stays in its county.
   - A race for an unexpired term keeps the regular seat's Office and gets its own race code. Two printed contests that would share one race stop the conversion.
   - Race and candidacy counts are unchanged in all five states, and no username changed. Seventy-eight offices for town roles still sit in a county because their rows never name the town, for example Erie County's four-letter town codes.
6. **Later.** Username update requests, public `/entities/:code` addresses that resolve aliases, fan lookup, and per-attribute update rules.

## Sequence registry

`10_01` (Person) and `10_02` (Office) are the approved starting assignments. Proposed: `10_03` Election (jurisdiction from scope, contest key in the code), `10_04` Candidacy (race from scope), and `10_05` Race (office and election from scope).
